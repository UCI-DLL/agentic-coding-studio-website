What is agentic coding?

The learner gives direction. The agent helps build.

Agentic coding is a way of making software by working back and forth with an AI coding agent. A learner explains what they want to create, then checks and guides the agent’s work. The agent can propose or change code, but the learner sets the goal, tests what was made, identifies problems, requests revisions, and remains responsible for the finished project.

  2. Plan

  4. Build
  5. Test
  6. Revise
  7. Reflect

The cycle repeats as ideas and evidence reshape the project.

Why this matters

The question is not simply whether youth use AI.

It is how they learn to use increasingly capable systems intentionally, critically, and creatively.

As AI changes how software is produced, productive struggle can move toward defining goals, communicating intent, evaluating generated work, debugging, revising, and making design decisions. Agentic Coding Studio studies how learning environments can keep those decisions with young people.

Research at a glance

Questions that connect design, learning, and context.

The project is investigating an emerging form of computational creation. These are areas of inquiry, not claims of results.

01 / Learning

How do youth learn to direct coding agents?

We study planning, prompting, testing, revision, and reflection as connected practices.

02 / Design

How can agents support thinking rather than replace it?

We examine scaffolds that make space for learner review, decisions, and ownership.

03 / Context

How do informal STEM settings shape participation?

We attend to educator facilitation, community, and differences across learners and settings.
